🎯 Key Concept

Voltage is the electrical pressure (like water pressure) that pushes electrons through a circuit.
Current is the flow rate of electrons (like water flow rate) through a conductor.

💡 Understanding the Water Analogy

Water Tank Height = Voltage: The higher the tank, the more pressure pushing water through pipes.

Water Flow = Current: The amount of water flowing per second through the pipe.

Pipe Width = Resistance: Narrow pipes restrict flow (high resistance), wide pipes allow more flow (low resistance).

⚡ Understanding Electron Flow

Voltage (V): The electrical potential difference that pushes electrons. Like pressure in water, higher voltage means stronger push.

Current (I): The rate of electron flow, measured in Amperes (A). One ampere = 6.24 × 10¹⁸ electrons per second!

Resistance (R): Opposition to electron flow, measured in Ohms (Ω). Thinner wires and certain materials have higher resistance.
