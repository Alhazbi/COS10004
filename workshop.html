    Tips for strong passwords

    • Use 16+ characters. Length matters more than tricks.
    • Mix lowercase, uppercase, digits and symbols.
    • Avoid words from your life: name, birthday, city, mobile number.
    • Use a different password for every account.
    • Use a password manager and let it generate random ones for you.
    • Check breaches at haveibeenpwned.com.
    Cryptography · Section 5

    Vigenère Cipher — Caesar's Bigger Brother

    Caesar uses one shift for the whole message. Vigenère uses a keyword: each letter of the keyword shifts the next letter of plaintext. Same letter in the message → potentially different ciphertext letters, breaking simple frequency analysis.

    Encrypt

    Decrypt

    How it works

    1. Repeat your keyword under the plaintext: ATTACKATDAWN / QATARQATARQA.
    2. For each pair of letters, shift the plaintext letter by the position of the key letter: A=0, B=1, … Z=25.
    3. Wrap around at Z back to A. Spaces and punctuation are kept untouched.
    4. Decryption is the same trick in reverse: shift the cipher letter back by the key letter's position.

    It looked unbreakable for 300 years — until Friedrich Kasiski figured out you could find the key length by spotting repeated patterns (Kasiski examination). Once you know the key length, the cipher reduces to several Caesar ciphers stacked side-by-side.
